# My Spec

Six lines. This is the whole skill we teach in Session 1.

Write these in Gemini first, then paste your answers here and hit
**Commit changes**. Replace the italic prompt after each dash — leave the dashes.

---

- **What it is:** *one sentence — if someone asked "what did you make?", this is the answer*
- **Who it is for:** *a specific person, not a category*
- **What it must do:** *the two or three things that, if missing, make it pointless*
- **What it must NOT do:** *what would stop you from sending someone the link*
- **What it looks like:** *sections in order, one accent color, one site it should feel like*
- **What "done" means:** *what you'd check before showing it to someone whose opinion you care about*

---

## Stuck? Questions to ask yourself

**What it is** — Keep it to one sentence. If it needs two, you are probably
building two things.

**Who it is for** — "Employers" is too vague to be useful. "A recruiter at a
company I actually want to work at, who will give this 30 seconds on their
phone" tells the model about length, layout, and what goes first.

**What it must do** — Not everything you *want*. The things that, if you took
them out, would make the whole thing pointless.

**What it must NOT do** — This one feels strange to answer and it is the most
useful line in the file. What does every other version of this get wrong? What
would embarrass you? What are you tired of seeing?

**What it looks like** — The line everyone writes worst, because it is tempting
to write moods. *"Forest aesthetic with beach vibes"* gives a model nothing to
build. Structure does: **sections in order, one accent color, one reference.**
Say "hero, story, three highlights, contact — deep green — lots of whitespace —
should feel like a clean personal site, not a resume" and you will get something
completely different.

**What "done" means** — You need a finish line, or you will keep fiddling until
the room clears out. What is the one thing that has to be true?

## A worked example

This is on purpose not a personal website — copy the *shape*, not the words.

- **What it is:** A page that shows which Cal Poly dining spots have the shortest lines right now.
- **Who it is for:** Me and my three roommates, on our phones, walking out of class.
- **What it must do:** Show every dining location, let anyone report a wait time, sort shortest first.
- **What it must NOT do:** Require an account. Take more than two taps to report a wait.
- **What it looks like:** One screen, no scrolling. Big tappable cards in a single column. Green for short waits, red for long. Feels like a weather app, not a spreadsheet.
- **What "done" means:** I can open it walking out of Building 52, know where to eat in under three seconds, and report a wait without thinking about it.

Notice there is nothing clever in there. It is specific, it is short, and every
line closes off a decision the model would otherwise make badly on its own.

## Why this file exists

You built this site twice today. Once from a one-line prompt, once from the six
lines above. The second one was better, and it was better because of this file —
not because the AI got smarter in between.

That is the whole idea. The spec is the skill. The tool will change three times
before you graduate.
